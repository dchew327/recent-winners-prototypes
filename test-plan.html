Research Questions

  • Does the Winners Widget register as trustworthy and credible at a glance, given anonymized win data?
  • Do users understand what the widget is showing — real wins, real games — without a player name attached?
  • Can users successfully interact with a winner card and navigate to the relevant game?

Task Plan

Study A — First Impression (5-Second Test)

EXPOSURE 5-Second Screen Flash
ScreenBig Winner Widget — Jackpocket home screen with widget visible (export from Figma: muyNmGOo5GoX05DK3vR1xR)
Duration5 seconds
Instructions"You're about to see a screen from a lottery app. It will only appear for 5 seconds. Look carefully — you won't be able to go back."
A1 Written Response
Question"What do you remember seeing on that screen?"
🔍 Internal: Open recall — what registers most strongly; is the widget prominent?
A2 Written Response
Question"What was the main purpose of the section showing recent wins?"
🔍 Internal: Comprehension — does the widget's social-proof purpose land?
A3 Written Response
Question"What stood out to you most — if anything?"
🔍 Internal: Visual salience — widget vs. other home elements
A4 Multiple Choice
Question"The wins shown in that section — how real did they feel to you?"
  • Very real — I believe these are actual wins
  • Somewhat real
  • Not sure — I couldn't tell
  • Not real — felt made up or generic
🔍 Internal: Trust/credibility signal for social proof hypothesis
A5 Written Response
Question"Did you notice any information was missing from the winner cards — like a name or location? What did you expect to see?"
🔍 Internal: Anonymization perception — does absence of name feel intentional (privacy-protecting) or suspicious (incomplete)?

Study B — Unmoderated Usability Test

⚠️ Prototype fidelity check required Confirm with Danny/Drew that the Figma prototype is interactive before launching Study B. If still static, reframe Tasks 3 and 4 as: "Point to where you would tap to [goal]. What would you expect to happen?"
B1 Navigate to URL + Verbal Response ~2 min
URLFigma prototype (public share from muyNmGOo5GoX05DK3vR1xR)
Scenario"Imagine you just downloaded a lottery app and you're opening it for the first time."
Instruction"Take a look at this home screen. Don't tap anything yet — just look around and tell me out loud what you notice."
SuccessParticipant notices the winners section without prompting
🔍 Internal: Unprompted widget discovery; what draws attention first; any immediate confusion
B2 Verbal Response (Think Aloud) ~2 min
Instruction"Looking at the section showing recent wins — what do you think it's showing you? Talk me through what you understand about it."
SuccessParticipant correctly identifies it as real, recent wins from other players
🔍 Internal: Comprehension of widget purpose; how users interpret the anonymized card format (state name vs. no player line)
B3 Verbal Response (Think Aloud) ~3 min
Scenario"You're thinking about playing Powerball this week."
Instruction"Show me how you'd use this screen to find Powerball. Please think out loud as you go."
SuccessParticipant taps a winner card or navigates to Powerball detail
🔍 Internal: Can users tap a winner card to navigate to a game? Do they understand the card is tappable?
B4 Verbal Response (Think Aloud) ~2 min
Scenario"You see a card that says someone in New York just won $50,000 on Powerball."
Instruction"What would you expect to happen if you tapped on that card? Go ahead and try it."
SuccessParticipant correctly anticipates or successfully reaches game detail page
🔍 Internal: Navigation comprehension; does "New York" on the card register as a trust signal or create confusion?
B5 Written Response ~2 min
Instruction"You just saw win cards that showed a state name (like 'New York') but no player name. Why do you think the app shows it that way?"
SuccessResponse indicates understanding that winner privacy is protected
🔍 Internal: Mental model for anonymization — protective / incomplete / suspicious?

Analysis Plan

Research Question Task(s) Metric Threshold
RQ1: Does the widget register as trustworthy? Study A — Q4, FU1 % rating "real"; mean trust score (1–7) ≥60% "real"; mean ≥5.0
RQ2: Do users understand the anonymous card format? Tasks B2, B5, FU2 Comprehension rate; mean ease score ≥80% correct interpretation; mean ≥5.0
RQ3: Can users tap through to a game? Tasks B3, B4 Task completion rate ≥80% success without prompting

Qualitative coding plan

  • Top friction themes from FU3 (severity: Low / Medium / High)
  • Anonymization perception: Protective / Incomplete / Suspicious / Neutral
  • Widget mental model: Social proof / Marketing / Random / Unsure
📊 Key cross-task signal to watch If trust scores (FU1) are high but anonymization perception (Task B5) skews "Incomplete" or "Suspicious," credibility is coming from something other than the win cards — investigate which element is actually driving it.

Language Decisions

Internal term Participant-facing equivalent Rationale
Widget "the section showing recent wins" / "that section" Customers don't think in widget terms
Winners Feed / carousel "the scrolling section" / "those win cards" Carousel is a UI term; describe functionally
Social proof "seeing that other people are winning" Concept, not a label
Jurisdictional filter Never use — describe outcome ("wins from your state") Engineering term
W-2G threshold Never use — irrelevant to UX research Tax/regulatory term
Big Winner "someone who won a large prize" Internal product label
Multi-state game "a lottery game available in multiple states, like Powerball" Describe, don't label
CTR Never use in participant copy Analytics jargon
Game detail page "the page for that game" Functional description
